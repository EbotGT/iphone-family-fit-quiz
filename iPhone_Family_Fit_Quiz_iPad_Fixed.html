Before you start: the three choices

These are the differences that matter most. Tap “More detail” if you want an explanation.

📱 iPhone 18 Pro

Traditional iPhone, 6.3-inch screen, lighter and easier to hold. Same A20 Pro chip and main Pro camera system as the Pro Max.

More detail

Best for someone who wants Pro-level speed/cameras but does not want the biggest phone. Apple rates it for up to 24 hours of typical use and 36 hours of video playback.

🔋 iPhone 18 Pro Max

Traditional iPhone, 6.9-inch screen. Bigger/heavier than the Pro, but has the strongest battery life of the three for heavy all-day use.

More detail

Same A20 Pro chip and Pro camera system as the 18 Pro. Apple rates it for up to 30 hours of typical use and 45 hours of video playback.

📖 iPhone Duo

A folding iPhone: 5.4-inch outer screen when closed and a 7.6-inch inner screen when opened. Built for multitasking and a mini-tablet feel.

More detail

It uses the same A20 Pro family chip, can show two apps side by side, supports Apple Pencil (USB-C), and can prop itself up. The tradeoff is that it uses Touch ID instead of Face ID and does not have the Pro models’ dedicated long-distance camera.

🕰️ What is StandBy?

StandBy turns your iPhone into a clock, photo frame, weather/calendar display, or widget screen when you set it down.

Why Duo is different

On a normal supported iPhone, StandBy is mainly designed for when the phone is charging and resting on its side. On iPhone Duo, Apple says StandBy can work on the inner or outer screen even when the phone is not charging. That makes it useful on a desk, counter, nightstand, hotel room, or while traveling.

🔭 What does “zoom camera” mean?

It means taking a clearer photo of something far away without walking closer — sports, wildlife, a person on a stage, mountains, signs, etc.

Which phones are better?

The 18 Pro and Pro Max have a dedicated 48MP long-distance camera. Duo has excellent main and wide cameras, but relies more on cropping/digital zoom for distant subjects.

🌡️ What are “thermals”?

Thermals simply means how well the phone handles heat. A hot phone can dim its screen or slow itself down to cool off.

Why it matters

Heat can build during GPS navigation, gaming, video recording, charging, hotspot use, AI tasks, or heavy use in hot weather. All three new models improve cooling, but the Pro models are especially designed for sustained performance.
